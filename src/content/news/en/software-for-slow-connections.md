Many business systems are designed for fast, stable internet. In much of Southern Africa, people use them on mobile data, on low-cost Android phones, during load-shedding and with a data bundle that has to last the month. Software that ignores this ends up unused. Here is what to look for.

## Ask how big the pages are

Every megabyte a system downloads costs your users time and money. Ask the supplier how large the main screens are and how long they take on a 3G connection. Good software loads its first screen in a few hundred kilobytes, not several megabytes.

## Check that it keeps working offline

Connections drop. A good system lets people keep working (taking attendance, recording a sale, filling in a form) and sends the data when the connection returns. Ask: "What happens if the network drops halfway through?"

## Test it on the phones your people really use

A system that runs well on a new laptop may struggle on an entry-level Android phone. Insist on testing with the devices your staff, students or customers actually carry.

## Look for WhatsApp and SMS, not only email

Many people read WhatsApp long before they open email. Notifications, approvals and reports that reach people on WhatsApp or SMS get acted on faster.

## Make sure you own your data

Whatever you choose, your data should stay yours, with a way to export it at any time. Ask where it is stored, how it is backed up and who can see it. Our [security page](/security) explains how we handle this.
