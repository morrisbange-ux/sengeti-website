Unpaid fees are one of the biggest worries for heads and bursars. Salaries, food and exam fees cannot wait, yet chasing families takes hours and can sour relationships. Most arrears are not refusals to pay. They come from forgotten balances, unclear invoices, and paying being harder than it should be. These six steps tackle those causes.

## 1. Send a clear invoice before the term starts

Families plan around what they know. An invoice that arrives before the term, lists each item and shows the due date gives parents time to plan. If you allow payment plans, put the instalments and dates on the invoice too.

## 2. Make paying from home easy

Every trip to the school office or the bank is a reason to put it off. Let parents pay from home with the methods they already use, such as EcoCash or a card, and send a receipt straight away so they know it arrived.

## 3. Let parents see their balance at any time

When a parent can check what they owe on their phone, or by sending a simple SMS, there are fewer arguments and fewer calls to the office. An up-to-date balance also stops two parents paying the same item twice.

## 4. Send friendly reminders automatically

A short, polite reminder a few days before a due date, and another soon after it, collects much of what would otherwise become arrears. Automating them means the reminders go out on time every time, without the bursar sending them one by one.

## 5. Know exactly who owes what

A debtors list sorted by amount and age shows where to focus. It also helps the head speak to families early and agree a plan before a small balance becomes a large one. Track sponsors and bursaries separately so they never appear as debts.

## 6. Keep the conversation respectful

Families who are struggling respond better to a private message and an offer of a plan than to a public list. Agree how and when the school contacts families about money, and write it down.
