Most teams don't need more staff. They need less manual work. If a task is done the same way more than twice, it can probably be automated. Here are the five places we usually start, because they save the most time for the least effort.

## 1. Approvals

Leave requests, purchase requests and payment approvals often live in email threads and WhatsApp chats, where nobody is sure who has them. An approval workflow sends each request to the right person, reminds them when it waits too long and keeps a record of every decision.

**Signs you need it:** people ask "has this been approved yet?" every week.

## 2. Data entry between systems

The same customer, invoice or student is typed into three places, and the three copies never match. Connecting those systems, or reading the data straight from the form or document, removes the retyping and the mistakes that come with it.

**Signs you need it:** someone spends part of every day copying between spreadsheets and systems.

## 3. Reports

Monthly reports built by hand are late, take days to collect and are out of date when they arrive. Automated reports pull the numbers from your systems and arrive on schedule, by email or on WhatsApp, with a live dashboard for anyone who wants more detail.

**Signs you need it:** the end of every month means a scramble for numbers.

## 4. Reminders and follow-ups

Invoices that are due, contracts that expire and customers who need a follow-up all depend on someone remembering. Automatic reminders by SMS, email or WhatsApp make sure nothing slips.

**Signs you need it:** money arrives late because nobody chased it.

## 5. Paper forms and registers

Paper is slow to fill in, easy to lose and easy to fake. Simple online forms that work on any phone, even on a slow connection, put the information straight into your system. Our [Sengeti Attendance](/products/attendance) platform replaced paper registers at a university in exactly this way.

**Signs you need it:** someone types up paper forms at the end of the week.

## Where to start

Pick the one process that annoys your team most, automate it, and measure the hours saved. Once it works, move on to the next.
